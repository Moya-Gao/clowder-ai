# ROUND 2 Mechanism Deep-Dive: Training & Inference Methods from Primary Sources

## TL;DR
- All five in-scope objects are verified against T0/T1 primary sources (technical-report PDFs, arXiv papers, official HF model cards): **DeepSeek-V4-Pro, OLMo 3/3.1, and GLM-5 have full primary-scope coverage; MiniMax-M3 is architecture/inference-only (its pre-train corpus + SFT + RL pipeline is confirmed absent from every primary source); Kimi K2.7-Code is limited to the deltas its model card explicitly states (built on K2.6, shared architecture, ~30% fewer thinking tokens).**
- **OLMo 3 is the single best worked example of end-to-end traceability** ("model flow"): the Dolma 3 corpus (Mix 5.9T → Dolmino 100B → Longmino 50B/100B), the Dolci post-train suite (SFT→DPO→RLVR), and OlmoTrace provenance (infini-gram over the full corpus) are all openly documented and anchorable.
- **Per-user personalization splits into two routes** — baking into weights (on-device fine-tuning) vs external memory/retrieval (RAG, frozen weights). Retrieval wins on auditability, deletability (GDPR), multi-user scaling and avoiding catastrophic forgetting; weights-baking wins on low-latency self-contained on-device inference and style adaptation.

## Key Findings
Temporal/object hygiene was applied throughout: no method was back-filled from a newer version onto an older one, and out-of-scope dimensions are explicitly marked "no public primary source found" rather than inferred. The cross-cutting 2026 theme is **efficient/sparse attention for million-token contexts**, but each family picked a distinct mechanism (DeepSeek hybrid CSA/HCA; MiniMax MSA on GQA; GLM-5 adopting DeepSeek's DSA; OLMo 3 deliberately staying dense for reproducibility; Kimi keeping the K2 MoE backbone + a vision encoder). **GRPO-family RL and MTP-based speculative decoding are now near-universal.**

## Details: dimension-by-dimension cross-family comparison

### Pre-train — architecture & attention
Each family chose a distinct route to million-token efficiency. **DeepSeek-V4** uses a proprietary hybrid (CSA + HCA), reaching 27% of V3.2's per-token FLOPs and 10% of its KV cache at 1M. **MiniMax-M3's MSA** keeps *real (uncompressed)* KV on a GQA backbone and selects blocks via a lightweight Index Branch (max-pool scoring → Top-k per group), with the local block always retained for stability — contrasting with DeepSeek's compression-based DSA (MLA + token-level top-k). **GLM-5** explicitly adopted DeepSeek's DSA via continued pre-training; its §2.1.2 ablation calls DSA "lossless by construction" versus sliding-window or linear (GDN/SimpleGDN) variants that degrade long-context retrieval. **OLMo 3** is the deliberate outlier — a plain dense transformer (no MoE, no exotic attention) prioritizing reproducibility; its 32B uses GQA (40 Q / 8 KV). **Kimi K2.5** keeps the K2 MoE backbone and bolts on MoonViT-3D for native multimodality.

Scale ranking: DeepSeek-V4-Pro (1.6T/49B) > Kimi K2.x (1T/32B) > GLM-5 (744B/40B) > MiniMax-M3 (~428B/23B) > OLMo 3 32B (dense). Optimizers: Muon (DeepSeek-V4; GLM-5 via "Muon Split"), MuonClip (Kimi).

### Pre-train — data & long-context
**OLMo 3 is uniquely fully documented**: Dolma 3 Mix (5.9T) → Dolmino mid-train (100B, math/code/thinking-heavy) → Longmino (50B/100B, long PDFs via olmOCR) extends context 8K→65K. **GLM-5** progressively extends 4K→200K across three mid-training stages (1T/500B/50B tokens). **DeepSeek-V4** (>32T) and **Kimi K2.5** (~15T mixed vision-text) report token budgets but not full mixes. **MiniMax-M3's corpus is not disclosed in any primary source.**

### Post-train — SFT / preference / RL
Two distinct philosophies. The **"specialist→generalist"** school: DeepSeek-V4 trains independent domain experts (SFT+GRPO) then merges them via on-policy distillation with reverse-KL over the full vocabulary. The **"sequential staged RL"** school: GLM-5 runs Reasoning→Agentic→General RL (GRPO + IcePop, KL removed) with on-policy cross-stage distillation to prevent forgetting. **OLMo 3** uses the cleanest canonical open pipeline (SFT→DPO→RLVR) with fully open Dolci datasets. **Kimi K2.5** contributes "zero-vision SFT" (text-only SFT activates vision) plus joint multimodal RL where visual RL measurably *improves* text benchmarks (MMLU-Pro +1.7, GPQA-Diamond +2.1). GRPO is the convergent RL backbone across DeepSeek/GLM; OLMo formalizes verifiable-reward RL (RLVR).

### Post-train — tool-use & agentic
Agentic post-training is now standard. **Kimi K2.5's** Agent Swarm/PARL trains an orchestrator while freezing subagents (sidesteps credit-assignment ambiguity and instability). **GLM-5's** asynchronous agentic RL scales >10K SWE/terminal/search environments on slime infra with a Token-in-Token-out gateway and DP-aware KV-cache routing. **DeepSeek-V4** trains a dedicated "agent" domain expert. **Kimi K2.7-Code** inherits K2's Interleaved Thinking + Multi-Step Tool Call (stated delta only).

### Inference-time techniques
- **Speculative decoding via MTP**: DeepSeek-V4 and GLM-5 both reuse MTP heads as built-in drafters; GLM-5 shares 3 MTP layers, achieving accept length 2.76 vs DeepSeek-V3.2's 2.55 at 4 speculative steps.
- **Sparse-attention kernels**: MiniMax-M3's MSA ships a co-designed kernel (exp-free Top-k, KV-outer ordering); GLM-5 switches the DSA indexer to deterministic `torch.topk` during RL to avoid non-determinism that crashed entropy.
- **Reasoning-mode switching**: DeepSeek-V4 (Non-think / Think High / Think Max), GLM-5 (Turn-level Thinking), Kimi K2.7-Code (thinking-only, ~30% fewer thinking tokens).
- **Quantization for serving**: GLM-5 W4A8 for Chinese chips + INT4 QAT; DeepSeek-V4 FP4+FP8 mixed checkpoints (MoE experts FP4, rest FP8).

## Per-User Personalization: Weights vs. External Memory/Retrieval

Two technical routes to make a model "remember/adapt to a specific user":
- **Route A — bake into weights** (on-device live-training / continuous fine-tuning of a small model).
- **Route B — external memory + retrieval** (RAG / memory system, weights frozen).

Source-quality note: most head-to-head comparisons are practitioner blogs (secondary); the strongest **primary** anchors are peer-reviewed/academic papers on catastrophic forgetting, machine unlearning, and RAG-based unlearning, cited inline below.

| Dimension | Route A: weights | Route B: retrieval/memory |
|---|---|---|
| **Auditability** | Low — knowledge entangled in parameters; hard to inspect what was learned. Provenance tooling (OlmoTrace via infini-gram) exists but needs full-corpus indexing | High — retrieved context is explicit and inspectable; you can see exactly which record drove an answer |
| **Updatability / deletability** | Hard — needs retraining or machine unlearning, which "often suffer from high computational demands, limited applicability, or the risk of catastrophic forgetting" (Wang et al., arXiv:2410.15267). Worse, forgotten knowledge "can be rapidly recovered via post-unlearning fine-tuning, even when fine-tuning is performed using data entirely unrelated to the unlearning" (arXiv:2506.01339), and Shumailov et al.'s "un-unlearning" shows capabilities can be reintroduced in-context after weight-level removal | Easy — edit/delete the record in the store. Wang et al. (arXiv:2410.15267): "By modifying the external knowledge base of RAG, we simulate the effects of forgetting without directly interacting with the unlearned LLM" (demonstrated on GPT-4o, Gemini, Llama-2-7b-chat-hf, PaLM 2) |
| **Catastrophic forgetting** | High risk — fine-tuning on new user data can overwrite prior knowledge; mitigations exist (e.g., Elastic Weight Consolidation, Kirkpatrick et al., PNAS 2017, doi:10.1073/pnas.1611835114: "slows down learning on certain weights based on how important they are to previously seen tasks") but do not eliminate it | None to the base model — weights frozen, so pre-trained capabilities are preserved |
| **Inference latency** | Lower per-query — no retrieval hop; self-contained; ideal for on-device low-latency | Higher — retrieval step + longer prompts add latency and token cost |
| **Privacy & multi-user isolation** | Strong if the small model is per-user and on-device (data never leaves device); but cloud fine-tuning embeds user data into weights that leave user control | Strong isolation achievable via per-user partitioned stores; but sensitive data sits in a retrievable index (jailbreak/exfiltration surface) |
| **Maintenance cost** | High — GPU-heavy retraining per user; does not scale to millions of distinct per-user models | Low — single frozen model + cheap per-user index updates; scales to many users |

**When each is better.**
- **Route B (retrieval/memory) is the sensible default** for per-user personalization at scale: it dominates on updatability/deletability (GDPR "right to be forgotten"), auditability, multi-user scaling, and avoiding catastrophic forgetting. Best when facts change frequently, deletion must be provably honored, and you serve many users from one model.
- **Route A (weights) is better** when: (1) you need low-latency, fully self-contained on-device inference with no retrieval infrastructure; (2) personalization concerns *style/behavior* rather than fact lookup; (3) the small model is genuinely per-user and on-device, maximizing privacy by never transmitting data. EWC/synaptic-intelligence and parameter-efficient adapters (LoRA) reduce but don't remove Route A's forgetting/cost downsides — and unlearning's fragility (un-unlearning, relearning attacks) is a real deletability risk.
- **Hybrid** (frozen base + retrieval for facts + a small per-user adapter for style) is increasingly the practical answer.

## Recommendations
1. **For mechanism-reuse and "how-it's-done" study, prioritize OLMo 3** — it is the only object with end-to-end traceable data→checkpoint lineage and OlmoTrace provenance; use it as the worked example.
2. **Treat MiniMax-M3 training as unavailable**: cite the MSA paper for attention/inference mechanisms only; never infer its corpus/SFT/RL. Keep the paper's 109B research-model efficiency numbers separate from the M3 product-card numbers.
3. **Keep Kimi K2.5 vs K2.7-Code strictly separated**: K2.5 = full recipe source (zero-vision SFT, joint RL, Agent Swarm/PARL, MoonViT-3D, MuonClip via K2 base); K2.7-Code = stated deltas only (built on K2.6, shared architecture, ~30% fewer thinking tokens). Do not copy K2.5/K2.6 training onto K2.7.
4. **For per-user personalization, default to retrieval/memory (Route B)**; reserve weights-baking (Route A) for on-device low-latency/style cases; adopt hybrid where both fact-freshness and style adaptation matter.
5. **Decision thresholds that would flip the choice**: if deletion-compliance latency or multi-user scaling dominate → Route B; if the deployment is offline/air-gapped on-device with no retrieval infra → Route A; if catastrophic forgetting on a small model is measured to exceed acceptable regression on held-out tasks → move that knowledge out of weights into retrieval.

## Caveats
- **DeepSeek-V4 token split** (33T Pro / 32T Flash) beyond the card's ">32T" wording is from secondary reviews (artgor/kili), explicitly labeled secondary; the card itself only states ">32T."
- **MiniMax MSA efficiency numbers differ by source and must not be conflated**: the MSA *paper* (109B research model, H800) reports 28.4× FLOPs reduction, 14.2× prefill, 7.6× decode at 1M; the *M3 model card* (M3 product vs M2) reports 9× prefill, 15× decode, 1/20 per-token compute. They concern different models and baselines. Also note the paper's research model is ~6B active, distinct from M3's ~23B active.
- **DeepSeek-V4's full technical-report PDF could not be machine-extracted** (Hugging Face Xet pointer); DeepSeek-V4 claims are anchored to the official model card text plus the report's confirmed contents reflected in the official HF discussion thread; deeper post-training nuances (e.g., "ten teacher models," full-vocabulary KL) appear in secondary reviews and are labeled as such where used.
- **MiniMax-M3's pre-train corpus, SFT, and RL pipeline are confirmed absent** from every primary source (model card README + MSA paper) — reported as "no public primary source found," per task scope, not inferred.
- This report makes **no layer-allocation ("which layer should this live in") judgment** — out of scope by instruction, as that requires internal system context not available here.
